# Scene-led edition: original production and licences

The apartment is a shared project-owned Blender reference, exported once as glTF for the real-time world. The source photographs and moving-camera film are rendered from that same scene. The footage uses genuine perspective camera movement, a moving door and an animated staged character; it is not a still-image zoom sequence. Finished assets ship; production scripts and capture frames do not.

Original surfaces, additional room details, character geometry, camera direction, sound synthesis and interface graphics are authored in the sceneplay sources. Owner-supplied images guide the low-key light, aged materials, close observation and restrained colour palette. No watermarked stock image or third-party character from those references is redistributed.

The retained Iris portrait, `assets/photos/iris.png`, is an existing project asset used consistently in the room. Its SHA-256 is `b35111ceedc42820e837a24d552d14b3869e026c53209f94772d97af67ba8253`. The project's pre-cleanup asset brief describes an editorial portrait with soft window light, a charcoal background and a charcoal turtleneck; that brief is preserved in the repository's current `docs/sceneplay/PROVENANCE.md`. The historical record does not identify a provider, receipt or separate external-image licence, so this notice does not infer new ownership or reuse terms.

Finished English voices are made locally using Kokoro ONNX and the official stock voice IDs `bf_alice`, `bm_daniel` and `bf_emma`, with no runtime inference, voice cloning or paid TTS. Output text and hashes are indexed in `assets/audio/manifest.json`. Kokoro-82M v1.0 and its official voice tensors are distributed under Apache 2.0 ([model card](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M)); kokoro-onnx and ONNX Runtime use MIT terms. The upstream Kokoro model card acknowledges Koniwa (CC BY 3.0) and SIWIS (CC BY 4.0) training material. The eSpeak NG phonemization library uses GPL v3 or later and its loader uses MIT; these are development tools. No weights, inference libraries, phonemizers or production executables ship in the game. Relevant upstream notice copies remain in the repository's `docs/sceneplay/licenses/`.

Three.js 0.169 is vendored locally under its MIT licence (vendor/LICENSE). There is no CDN or remote runtime dependency. glTF loading helpers are from the same version. The game requires WebGL 2, which current iPhone Safari and desktop browsers support. Physical-device performance remains a test requirement.

The original cinematic score, rain, latch, room clock and footstep sounds are locally synthesized. FFmpeg and Blender are offline production tools, not requirements for playing. All runtime files are static and use relative paths.

The updated officer is an original articulated procedural model. Face, eyelids, uniform, hands and linked notebook pose are authored in `src/officer.js`. Speech movement and the phone waveform use RMS envelopes extracted locally from the existing finished recordings; `assets/audio/motion.json` records those recordings' hashes. This is speech-timed animation, not phoneme or performance capture. Neither the character nor the new interface assets were generated through a paid media service. Paper, hardware, ink marks and transitions use original CSS and SVG.

The project owner retains the game's overall distribution-licence decision. This asset notice does not relicense the game or claim exclusive copyright in synthetic output. The shipped Three.js MIT notice is available locally at `vendor/LICENSE`.
